# Anthropic IPO Updates 2026
*Researched 2026-08-18 · 18 sources*

## Executive Summary

Anthropic is genuinely on the path to a public listing. The company confidentially submitted a draft S-1 registration statement to the SEC on 1 June 2026, three days after closing a $65 billion Series H round at a $965 billion post-money valuation. Press reporting points to a Nasdaq debut as early as October 2026 at a valuation near $2 trillion, which would be the largest IPO ever — but that figure comes from Anthropic's investors talking to reporters, not from the company, which has confirmed no date, no valuation, no exchange, and no share price. The gap between what Anthropic has said and what its backers are telling the press is the single most important thing to understand about this story.

**Key takeaways**
- **Confirmed by Anthropic:** a confidential draft S-1 filed 1 June 2026, and a $65B Series H at $965B post-money. That's it.
- **Reported but not confirmed:** an October 2026 Nasdaq listing, a ~$2 trillion target, Goldman Sachs / Morgan Stanley / JPMorgan as lead underwriters, and a raise above $60B.
- Anthropic's self-reported run-rate revenue crossed **$47 billion in May 2026** — up from roughly $19B in March — though this is unaudited and the accounting is contested.
- Employees were offered a chance to cash out in an April tender offer at a $350B valuation and **largely refused to sell**, betting the IPO would be worth more. They were right so far.
- OpenAI filed its own confidential S-1 a week after Anthropic's, targeting the same Q4 2026 window — the two are effectively racing.

## Background

A few things make this listing unusual, and they're worth having straight before the findings.

- **A confidential S-1 is not an IPO.** It's a draft registration statement submitted privately to the SEC so the regulator can review and comment before anything becomes public. It buys the company the *option* to list. Companies file and then don't list all the time.
- **Anthropic is a Delaware Public Benefit Corporation** (the filing entity is "Anthropic, PBC"). Its board is legally required to balance shareholder returns against a stated public mission, rather than maximising returns alone.
- **A trust controls the board, not the shareholders.** The [Long-Term Benefit Trust](https://www.anthropic.com/news/the-long-term-benefit-trust) is an independent five-member body holding a special "Class T" share class, with the power to elect a majority of Anthropic's board. Public investors would be buying into a company they cannot control.
- **Nobody can check the numbers yet.** Every revenue figure below is company-provided or analyst-estimated. Audited financials only become public when the S-1 does.

## Key Findings

### 1. What has actually happened

- **1 June 2026** — Anthropic [confirmed](https://www.anthropic.com/news/confidential-draft-s1-sec) it "confidentially submitted a draft registration statement on Form S-1" for a proposed IPO of its common stock. The company's own wording is deliberately non-committal: it gives Anthropic "the option to go public after the SEC completes its review," and the offering "will depend on market conditions and other factors." The number of shares and the price "have not yet been set."
- The announcement was published under SEC Rule 135 — a technical rule that lets a company acknowledge a planned offering without legally making one. That formality is why the statement says so little.
- **3 June** — Morgan Stanley, Goldman Sachs and JPMorgan Chase were reported as lead underwriters ([TechTimes](https://www.techtimes.com/articles/317845/20260605/anthropic-ipo-picks-goldman-sachs-morgan-stanley-jpmorgan-revenue-accounting-question-looms.htm), [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/anthropic-said-pick-morgan-stanley-175802678.html)). Anthropic has not confirmed this.
- **July 2026** — banks began scheduling pre-IPO investor meetings ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/15/anthropic-ipo-banks-investor-meetings.html)), a step that typically precedes a formal roadshow by several weeks.
- **13 August 2026** — CFO Krishna Rao began early investor meetings, described as investor education rather than a roadshow. Per [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/13/anthropic-cfo-early-ipo-meetings-valuation.html), Rao **did not discuss valuation** in these sessions.

### 2. What Anthropic's own people say

- Remarkably little. Neither Dario Amodei nor the company has publicly named a listing date, valuation, or exchange.
- Per the [Financial Times reporting relayed by PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2026/anthropic-could-seek-2-trillion-valuation-in-record-ipo/), "senior executives have not established an IPO valuation target, even in private conversations." That is a notable contrast with the $2T figure circulating in the press.
- Amodei's public messaging in August has been about credibility, not markets. On 16 August he [wrote](https://thenextweb.com/news/dario-amodei-ai-public-trust-cure-cancer-ipo): "The most accurate criticism of AI companies, including Anthropic, is that we haven't yet delivered on our big promises to benefit the world," adding that "saying that AI will cure cancer is more a cliché than it is inspiring… The thing that will work is actually curing cancer."
- *Inference, not sourced fact:* an AI-safety-branded company heading into a listing has obvious reasons to manage expectations rather than hype them. Read the restraint as strategy, not indifference.

### 3. What the money says

- **Series H** ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h), 28 May 2026): $65B raised at $965B post-money. Leads were Altimeter, Dragoneer, Greenoaks and Sequoia; co-leads included Capital Group, Coatue, D1, GIC, ICONIQ and XN. [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/28/anthropic-raises-65-billion-nears-1t-valuation-ahead-of-ipo/) described it as likely the last private round before listing.
- **Valuation ramp in 2026:** $380B in February (Series G, $30B) → $965B in May. That is a 2.5x increase in roughly three months.
- **Revenue:** run-rate crossed **$47B in May 2026**, per Anthropic. [Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/anthropic/) estimates $65B by July. Anthropic's backers told the FT they expect $100–120B annualised by year-end.
- **Revenue mix** ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/anthropic/)): 70–75% API, 10–15% subscriptions. Eight of the Fortune 10 are customers; 1,000+ customers spend over $1M a year.
- **Costs are the other half of the story.** The Series H announcement discloses compute commitments including 5 gigawatts with Amazon, 5 gigawatts of Google/Broadcom TPU capacity, and GPU access from SpaceX, plus chip supply deals with Micron, Samsung and SK hynix. Sacra estimates ~$80B in cloud infrastructure costs through 2029.

### 4. How insiders are getting liquidity today

- Anthropic ran an employee **tender offer** opening late February 2026, letting current and former staff with at least a year's tenure sell shares. It priced at a **$350B pre-money valuation**.
- The deal **undershot**: investors had lined up $5–6B and couldn't get it, because employees refused to sell ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-04-08/anthropic-completes-tender-offer-but-employees-hold-onto-shares), [Benzinga](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/private-markets/26/04/51740588/anthropics-tender-offer-falls-short-of-its-estimated-6-billion)).
- Crucially, this wasn't a pricing complaint — staff simply expected the shares to be worth more later ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/news/articles/employees-at-mega-ipo-candidates-are-opting-out-of-tender-offers-in-a-champagne-problem) calls it a "champagne problem" across mega-IPO candidates).
- **That bet has paid off so far.** April's tender priced at $350B; the Series H a month later priced at $965B — roughly 2.75x for anyone who held.

### 5. What has to be true before it lists

- **SEC review must complete.** The draft is confidential; a public S-1 with audited financials must be filed before a roadshow.
- **The governance question needs an answer.** Public-market investors buy shares in a company whose board majority is elected by the Long-Term Benefit Trust. Reporting suggests institutions are split — the PBC structure appeals to ESG-mandated capital, while others view the Trust as deliberately disenfranchising public shareholders.
- **The accounting needs to survive audit.** Sacra notes API revenue is reported on a **gross basis** through cloud resellers, which inflates headline figures relative to competitors reporting net. Underwriter-stage coverage flagged a "revenue accounting question" from the start.
- **Profitability remains unresolved.** Anthropic reportedly projected a first quarterly operating profit of ~$559M in Q2 2026, while warning it may not stay profitable across the full year as data-centre spending ramps. Analyst estimates put sustained profitability in 2028–29.

### 6. The OpenAI comparison

- OpenAI [confirmed](https://openai.com/index/openai-submits-confidential-s-1/) its own confidential S-1 on **8 June 2026**, one week after Anthropic's — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/06/08/following-anthropic-openai-files-confidentially-for-ipo/) framed it explicitly as following Anthropic.
- OpenAI's last private valuation was **$852B**, below Anthropic's $965B — a reversal of the historical ordering. Its targeted window is September–November 2026, overlapping Anthropic's reported October.
- Same three banks are reported to lead both deals.
- **Structural difference:** OpenAI had to complete a complex non-profit-to-PBC restructuring to get here. Anthropic has been a PBC from the start, giving it a cleaner cap table — but it swapped that for the Trust's control over the board.

## Disagreements & Open Questions

- **The $2 trillion figure is investor talk, not company guidance.** Six Anthropic backers gave it to the FT; executives reportedly have no target even privately. Treat it as sell-side enthusiasm.
- **Some reporting is arithmetically wrong.** Multiple outlets describe the projected $100–120B year-end revenue as "more than tenfold" growth from May's $47B. It's roughly 2.5x. Be sceptical of secondary coverage that can't do the division.
- **Revenue figures conflict badly.** Sacra estimates $65B ARR in July; backers project $100–120B by December; some aggregator coverage still cites $20–26B for full-year 2026. These cannot all be right, and none are audited.
- **A serious profitability challenge exists.** Ed Zitron [argues](https://www.wheresyoured.at/anthropics-profitability-swindle/) (21 May 2026) that Q2's projected profit was manufactured by temporarily suppressed costs — specifically a discounted SpaceX compute deal at $1.25B/month — and relies on non-GAAP metrics unverifiable while private. He also flags that CFO statements about "exceeding $5 billion" in March don't reconcile with $30B ARR claims. *This is one analyst's view, not established fact — but the reconciliation question is real and the S-1 will answer it.*
- **Is 20x sales defensible?** [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/ronschmelzer/2026/08/14/anthropic-at-2-trillion-is-ai-entering-bubble-territory/) calls it "expensive, certainly, but not entirely absurd," while warning that unlike traditional software, every query costs real compute and power — and that cheaper open-weight models could commoditise routine work.
- **Unknown:** ticker, exchange (Nasdaq is reported, not confirmed), float size, lock-up terms, and whether retail investors get meaningful allocation.

## What This Means

- **Nothing is buyable yet.** There is no ticker and no price. Any service offering "pre-IPO Anthropic shares" to retail deserves heavy scepticism.
- **The date to watch is the public S-1 filing**, not the October chatter. That document brings audited financials, real customer-concentration disclosure, and the compute commitments in full. It settles most of the arguments above.
- **Anchor on $965B, not $2T.** The former is a completed transaction; the latter is what people who already own shares hope buyers will pay.
- **If you write about this, the employee tender is the best detail.** Insiders with the most information declined to sell at $350B, and were vindicated within a month. That's a stronger signal than any banker's valuation.
- **Two frontier labs listing in one quarter is the real story.** Whichever prices first sets the comparable for the other, and both are effectively asking public markets to fund compute buildouts that don't pay back until 2028–29.
