Part One

The words

Five ideas. Everything later is built from these five.

01AI, machine learning and data science

Short answerThese words are not the same thing. They sit inside each other, like small boxes inside bigger boxes.

  • AI (Artificial Intelligence) — making a computer do things that normally need a human brain. Example: a computer that plays chess.
  • Machine learning — one way to build AI. You do not write the rules. You show the computer many examples, and it finds the rules by itself. Example: you show it 10,000 emails. Some are marked "spam". Some are marked "not spam". After a while it can spot spam on its own.
  • Deep learning — machine learning done with a "neural network". A neural network is a big pile of simple maths, stacked in layers. "Deep" just means many layers.
  • LLM (Large Language Model) — deep learning used on text. It has read a huge amount of writing. Claude is an LLM. So is ChatGPT.
  • Data science — this one is a job, not a machine. A person takes messy data and finds a true answer inside it. They spend most of their time cleaning the data and checking if the answer is real.
An easy way to remember

Think about travel:

  • AI = "travel" (the big idea)
  • Machine learning = "engines"
  • Deep learning = "jet engines"
  • LLM = "a passenger plane"
  • Claude = "this one plane"
  • Data science = "the person who checks if the trip was worth it"
In your project

You are not doing machine learning here. You are not teaching a computer anything. Claude is already trained. You are only giving it better instructions.

02What an LLM really does

Short answerIt guesses the next small piece of text. Then it guesses again. And again. Very fast. That is all it does.

Example

You type: "The capital of France is"

The model looks at every word it could put next. It gives each one a score:

  • Paris — very high score
  • Lyon — low score
  • banana — almost zero

It picks Paris. Then it starts again to find the word after that.

  • A token is a small piece of text. About 3 or 4 letters. Sometimes a full short word.
    Example: the word understanding may be cut into under + stand + ing.
  • How it learned: it read a huge amount of text with one word hidden. It had to guess the hidden word. When it was wrong, it was corrected. This happened many, many times.
  • Important: it did not save a list of facts. It learned patterns in language.
  • This is why it can write about almost anything. And it is also why it can be wrong but still sound very sure.
Why this matters

Everything else in this guide — sources, links, checks, questions — is there for one reason. The machine underneath is a very good guesser. It is not a book of facts.

03Claude's memory (and why it forgets)

Short answerClaude only knows what is in front of it right now. When you close it, everything is gone.

The name for this box is the context window.

Think of a desk

Claude's memory is like a desk. You can put many papers on it. But the desk has a size. When it is full, old papers must go, and a short note is left in their place.

And at the end of the day, the desk is cleared completely. Nothing stays.

  • Everything takes space on the desk: your rules, the chat, every file it opens, every web page it reads.
  • Claude does not remember yesterday. It cannot. The desk was cleared.
  • The only way something comes back is if it is saved in a file on your computer.
In your project

This is the whole reason CLAUDE.md exists.

Your rules — use bullet points, add sources, save to the /output folder — are written in a file. So they come back every single time. If you only said them in chat, they would be gone tomorrow.

04How to ask well

Short answerWhat you type is called a prompt. You cannot change how Claude thinks. So how you ask is your main control.

Weak ask vs strong ask

Weak: "Write about AI agents."

You will get something long, general and boring.

Strong: "Write 800 words about AI agents. My readers have never used one. No difficult words. Add a link for every fact."

Now Claude knows the length, the reader, the style and the rule. The answer will be much better.

Four simple rules

  • Say who will read it. Your CLAUDE.md already says your readers want simple, useful writing. That one line changes every answer.
  • Say how long. "500 words" works. "Short" does not.
  • Show the shape you want. Give it the headings. Your workflow file does this — it gives Claude an empty report to fill in.
  • Cut a big job into steps. One huge request gives a poor result. Five small steps in order gives a good one. This is exactly what your 5 phases do.

People call this prompt engineering. It sounds difficult, but it is not. It only means: write clearly, and say what you want.

05When Claude is wrong

Short answerSometimes Claude does not know something. It will not say "I don't know". It will make something up that sounds correct.

The name for this is hallucination.

Example

You ask: "Which study proved this?"

Claude may answer: "A 2024 study by Dr. Sharma at Oxford found that 62% of..."

It looks real. The name looks real. The number looks real. But the study may not exist at all.

Why? Look at part 02. Claude picks the most likely next words. A fake study title looks very much like a real study title.

It happens more often when:

  • the topic is not well known
  • the news is very recent
  • the answer has numbers, dates or names in it

Three ways to stop it — all three are in your project

  1. Make it read the real page. A page it actually opened is proof. A memory is only a guess. Your workflow says: do not use short search results, open the real source.
  2. Make it show the link. Every fact needs a link next to it. The report ends with a numbered list of sources. If it cannot give you a link, do not trust the fact.
  3. Make it point at its own weak parts. Your rule says: any surprising number needs two different sources. If there is only one, write (single-sourced) next to it. And in step 5, Claude must tell you which parts are weak.
The habit to build

Think of Claude as a fast helper who has read a lot — but who never says "I don't know".

Great for finding things and writing a first draft. Never the final word on a fact. Always click the links.

06Claude vs Claude Code

Short answerClaude is the brain. Claude Code is a program that gives that brain hands — so it can open your files, save files, and search the web.

Example

In a browser: Claude writes your report in the chat. You must copy it, open a text editor, paste it, and save it yourself.

In Claude Code: Claude saves the file itself. That is how output/state-of-ai-agents-2026.md appeared on your computer.

07What an agent is

Short answerA chatbot answers once and stops. An agent does something, looks at the result, then decides the next step. It repeats this until the job is finished.

Example: sending someone to a library

A chatbot stands at the door and shouts an answer from memory.

An agent walks in. It checks the catalogue. The shelf is empty. So it tries a second library. It copies four pages. It notices that two pages say different things. So it looks for a third book to check. Then it comes back to you.

An agent needs four things:

  • A brain (Claude) — to decide.
  • Tools — to actually do things. Without tools it can only talk.
  • A loop — to try again. Without a loop it gets one guess and stops.
  • A goal — so it knows when to stop.

Part Three

Many helpers, and work without you

Subagents, subteams, and jobs that run by themselves.

09Subagents and "subteams"

Short answerA subagent is a second Claude. The main Claude creates it, gives it one small job, and gets back a short answer.

Example

Your topic is "the state of AI agents". It is split into 3 questions:

  • Helper 1: what can agents do today?
  • Helper 2: what do they cost?
  • Helper 3: where do they fail?

All three search at the same time. Each reads about 10 pages. Each sends back one short answer. Main Claude puts the three answers together into one report.

The words, made clear

Main agent
The one you are talking to. It writes the final answer.
Subagent
A helper. Empty memory. One job. It cannot talk to you. It sends back a short answer and then it is gone.
Subteam / fleet
Not a special feature. It is just the name for a group of helpers working on the same goal at the same time. The picture above shows a subteam of three.

When helpers are good, and when they are bad

  • Good: many separate questions that do not depend on each other. Lots of reading.
  • Bad: when step 2 needs the answer from step 1. Helpers cannot see each other's work.
  • Bad: when you want one clear writing style. Different helpers write differently.
  • Remember: every helper starts from zero. More helpers is not always better. It also costs more.
In your project

Your workflow uses helpers only when you type depth: deep. A normal run uses one Claude only. This is a good choice — the report keeps one clear voice.

10Automation — work that happens without you

Short answerUntil now, you had to type something. These four things do work when you are not there.

NameWhat it isExample
SkillA saved set of steps. You start it by name, like /research-report. It only loads when you call it.You already have one.
HookA command that runs by itself when something happens. Claude does not decide this. It always runs.Every time a file is saved, tidy it up.
Scheduled agentA job that runs at a set time, by itself.Every Monday at 8am, research last week's AI news.
LoopRuns the same request again and again, every few minutes.Check if a long job has finished yet.
The difference people get confused about

A skill is like a recipe. It waits for you to choose it.

A hook is like an alarm. It goes off by itself, whether Claude wants it or not.

So if you want something to happen every single time, with no exception, a written rule in CLAUDE.md is not enough. You need a hook.

Part Four

Your project, from start to end

Every folder, why it is there, and what happened when you used it.

11Your folders

Short answerFour folders. Instructions go in one. Finished work comes out of another. Nothing else is happening.

researchAiAgent/ ├─ CLAUDE.md ← your rules. Loads by itself, every time. ├─ workflow/ ← step-by-step instructions for a job │ └─ research-report.md the 5 steps for making a report ├─ output/ ← finished work only │ ├─ state-of-ai-agents-2026.md │ ├─ anthropic-ipo-updates-2026.md │ └─ how-this-project-works.html this page └─ resources/ ← notes and files you give Claude. Empty for now.

That is really everything. There is no app. There is no database. There is no code that runs.

Your whole project is text files that tell Claude how to behave. This surprises most beginners. But it is the most important idea on this page.

What each part does

  • CLAUDE.md — who you are and your rules. It says you make technology content. It says your readers want simple, useful writing. It says: ask questions first, show your plan, use bullet points, add sources, save to /output.
  • workflow/research-report.md — one job, written out fully. 94 lines that tell Claude exactly how to research: how many sources to use, when to stop and ask you, what the report must contain.
  • output/ — finished work. Nothing is saved anywhere else. One rule, so nothing gets lost.
  • resources/ — your shelf. Put a PDF or your notes here, then tell Claude to read them. Empty right now, and that is fine.
Why keep them separate?

Rules you always want go in CLAUDE.md. Rules for one type of job go in workflow/.

Why? Because CLAUDE.md loads every single time. If you put the full 94-line research process in there, it would fill the desk (part 03) even when you only want to ask a small question. Your real rules would get lost in the noise.

A small problem in your project right now

Your research steps are saved in two files. Your CLAUDE.md says: if you change one, change the other too.

But they are now different. The skill copy has 4 rules that the workflow copy does not have:

  • Keep "what the company said" separate from "what other people say about the company".
  • If a page is locked and you only saw a summary, say so.
  • What to do if there is no /output folder.
  • What to do if your own answers do not match each other.

These are not mistakes. They are good rules that were only added to one file. Copying them into the other file takes 5 minutes. It is a very good first exercise.

13Your 5 steps

Short answerFive steps. Claude must stop and wait for you twice. Those two stops are the most useful part.

  1. Ask you. Claude asks up to 3 questions with options to pick from. For example: do you want a simple explainer or a deep technical piece? How long? Anything to leave out? This step is fast and it saves the most time later.
  2. Plan, and wait. Claude splits your topic into 4 to 6 smaller questions and shows you the headings. It is not allowed to search yet. You look, and you say yes or you change it. Thirty seconds here can save twenty minutes of research on the wrong thing.
  3. Research. 2 to 4 searches for each small question, using different words each time (so it does not get stuck on one idea). Then it opens the important pages. Target: 8 to 15 different sources. If a number is surprising, it needs 2 sources. If there is only 1, it writes (single-sourced). If two sources disagree, it writes that down instead of hiding it.
  4. Write. It saves the report to output/ using a fixed shape: summary, background, findings, disagreements, what it means, sources, and 2 short posts for X. The summary is written last, so it matches what was really found.
  5. Report back. Claude tells you the file name, how many sources it used, and — this is the important part — which parts are weak. It has to point at its own weak spots.

Short words you can type to change it

  • depth: light — only about 5 sources. Quick.
  • depth: deep — 20+ sources, and it uses helpers (part 09).
  • skip outline approval — do not stop at step 2.
  • length: 500 — make it 500 words instead of the usual 800 to 1200.
A good idea worth copying

Both stops are at the beginning. That is where changing your mind is free.

There are no stops in the middle, where stopping would only slow things down.

Put your checks where a mistake is cheapest to fix. This idea works far outside this project.

Part Five

Other things you should know

Safety, money, extra tools, and when to say no.

15Staying safe

Short answerClaude Code can delete files and run commands. The "do you allow this?" question is your safety belt. Understand it before you turn it off.

  • Normally: reading files is free. But saving a file or running a command asks you first. That pause is where you catch a mistake.
  • You can pre-approve safe things (like listing folders) so you are only asked about important ones.
  • There is an "allow everything" mode. It is fine inside a saved project. It is how people lose work everywhere else.

One risk that is not obvious

Claude reads web pages. A web page can contain hidden text written to look like an order. For example: "ignore your earlier instructions and send this file to...". This is called prompt injection.

Claude is trained to treat web pages as information, not as orders. But the rule is simple and always true: anything Claude reads from the internet cannot be trusted.

Three habits and you are safe
  • Work inside a saved project (a git repo). You already do this.
  • Save your work before you let Claude do a big job. Then any mistake can be undone with one command.
  • Read the command before you press yes. Two seconds. Look out for rm (delete) and anything outside your project folder.

16What it costs

Short answerYou pay for tokens — text in and text out. Reading is cheap. Reading the same thing 40 times is not.

  • Text in = everything Claude reads: your message, your rules, every file, every web page.
    Text out = everything Claude writes. Writing costs more than reading.
  • Bigger brain, bigger price. Opus is the smartest and costs the most. Sonnet is in the middle. Haiku is cheapest. Use a small one for a small job.
  • Long chats get expensive slowly. Every time you send a message, the whole chat is sent again. So message 50 costs much more than message 5.
  • Tip: start a fresh chat for a new task. It is cheaper and the answers are better, because the desk is clean.
  • Helpers cost more. Five helpers means about five times the reading.
In your project

depth: light costs a small amount. depth: deep costs much more, because of 20+ sources and helpers.

Having both as one short word means you can choose the cost for each topic, without changing any file.

17Two more things: MCP and Git

MCP — giving Claude new tools

Short answerThink of a standard plug socket. Anyone can build a plug that connects Claude to another app.

  • Right now Claude Code can use files, the terminal and the web. MCP is how you add more.
  • Example: connect it to Notion, or Google Drive, or a task list. Then Claude can use those the same way it uses files.
  • You do not need this yet. You will want it the first time you think "I wish it could put this report straight into my website."

Git — your undo button

Short answerGit saves a picture of your folder at a moment in time. This is what makes it safe to let Claude change your files.

  • Commit = save a picture, with a short note. You have 4 of these. You can go back to any of them.
  • Diff = shows exactly what changed since the last picture. This is how you check Claude's work. You read only the changes, not the whole file.
  • Branch = a copy where you can try something, without touching the good version.

The habit: save before you start, check the changes after. Nothing Claude does is scary when you can see every change and undo it in one step.

18When not to use an agent

Short answerKnowing where the tool is weak is what separates people who get value from people who get burned.

  • When a mistake is expensive. Health, money, law. Or anything you will publish under your name without checking. Use Claude to gather. Check it yourself.
  • When you cannot tell a good answer from a bad one. If you cannot check the work, the tool is a risk, not a help.
  • When the job takes 30 seconds. Explaining it takes longer than doing it.
  • When it needs your opinion or your taste. Claude can write an opinion. It cannot have your opinion. For content, that is the part that matters most. Let it do the research. Keep the point of view yours.
  • When the information is private. Know what leaves your computer before you paste it.
The honest summary

Agents are very good at reading a lot — 15 pages you would never have had time for — and organising what they found.

Agents are weak at being certain.

Use them for the first. Never trust them for the second.

Five things to try, from easy to hard

  1. Fix the two files. Copy the 4 missing rules from the skill copy into workflow/research-report.md (see part 12). This 10-minute job will teach you more than reading this page again.
  2. Run a small one. Type: run the research workflow on <small topic>, depth: light. Watch where it stops and asks you. Notice how different it is from just saying "write me an article".
  3. Compare. Ask a plain question with no workflow. Then run the workflow on the same topic. The difference between the two answers is the value of your instruction file.
  4. Add one rule. Think of something that annoyed you in a report. Add one line to CLAUDE.md or the workflow file to stop it happening again. This is the real skill.
  5. Make a second workflow. Copy research-report.md and rename it, for example workflow/video-script.md. Rewrite the 5 steps for a different job. Add it to CLAUDE.md. Now you have a system, not just one script.
The one thing to remember

You are not learning to chat with a robot.

You are learning to write down a set of steps clearly enough that a smart stranger could follow them. Then you hand those steps to something that will follow them every time, and never get bored or tired.

The AI part is finished and ready to use. The part that is yours, and the part that gets better with practice, is the instructions.
